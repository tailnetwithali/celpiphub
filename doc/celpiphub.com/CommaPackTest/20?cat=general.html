MAN: One of the growing concerns among Canada’s workforce is the fact that on average, it is aging. This will have major impacts throughout Canada.

However, a more recent report suggests the opposite, particularly in Alberta. According to this report, the working population will actually remain relatively young in the next few decades. Moreover, it will also become highly diverse.

If we consider the cities of Edmonton and Calgary, both among the largest in the province, they feature the highest labor force participation rate in the country as compared to similar large cities.

This is according to the Statistics Canada report. The report defines this rate as the percentage of people working or looking for a job who are aged 15 years or older. For Alberta, the rate is at seventy two percent and is expected to remain constant through 2036. When compared to the national average, the rate is sixty two percent.

But one of the major factors that is contributing to the relative youth of the labor force is immigration. And this will significantly affect Alberta as the report expects immigrants to increasingly choose to settle in Alberta.

The current rate of immigrants working in either Calgary or Edmonton is 31%, but is projected to increase to 43% by 2036. According to the report, large urban areas will experience high rates of immigration of workers as well as younger adults from other provinces, which will offset the effects of aging workers.

Moreover, the ratio of labor force participants to retired workers will be as much as five to one in Calgary and Edmonton. Whereas in other major metropolitan centers like Montreal, it will be much lower.

This is not surprising to Anurag Rao, an economics professor with University of Calgary. According to Rao, most immigrants will settle in Alberta’s cities and this will be the same throughout the country.

This is because immigrants more often like to find better job opportunities and services. When it comes to Alberta, Rao believes that it’s good for the province to receive many immigrants.

However, when it comes to actual jobs, Rao does call into question that the report is unrealistic. He notes that just because more people are participating in the labor force, this doesn’t mean that more people are actually working.

He cautions that care must be taken because if the participation rate goes up and conditions are not suitable for absorbing these workers, then it’s likely that unemployment will increase and this will limit economic benefits for all concerned. He cites the example of people who are looking for jobs.

According to him, if these people can’t find work or end up working part time or earning low wages, This will stifle economic benefits for the province. To better be able to deal with this problem, there is a need to create more jobs.

Thus, the creation of more jobs, particularly full time jobs will be really good for the economy and will also help to integrate immigrants more easily into Canadian society.
