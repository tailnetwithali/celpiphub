Voice5:

WOMAN: Thank you for being here today. I’ve been researching the history of the Klondike Gold Rush. I’m really fascinated about how it changed Canada’s Yukon Territory.

MAN: Well, the Klondike Gold Rush was phenomenal because like the California Gold Rush, it brought a large number of people into one region. I’ve been researching the history and writing books on the early history of the Yukon Territory.

WOMAN: Wow. Well, let’s start with what the area was like at the beginning. What was going on at that time?

MAN: Well, it didn’t happen until 1898, and the Klondike region was primarily wilderness, populated by indigenous tribes. Most of the tribes were aware that gold was present in the region, but it was not valued by them.

WOMAN: So which tribes lived there before Europeans?

MAN: Let’s see. There were several and included the native Tlingit and Tagish tribes. But in the area of the Gold Rush in the Yukon Valley lived the Han people.

WOMAN: The Han people? What were they like?

MAN: Well, they were semi nomadic people who did hunting and fishing between the Klondike and Yukon Rivers. Interestingly, the Han were not aware of the extent of the gold in the area.

WOMAN: So if the Han were unaware of gold, how was it discovered?

MAN: It wasn’t really until 1883 when gold deposits were found along the Yukon River. 3 Years later, gold was found along the 40 Mile River and this led to the establishment of Forty Mile City. But it’s important to note that the total amount of gold found at that time was not significant enough to spark a rush.

WOMAN: Did anyone make any claims at that time?

MAN: For the deposits along the Klondike, no. No claims were made.

WOMAN: So what was it that sparked the gold rush then?

MAN: It wasn’t until August 1896 when American George Carmack and his party found a huge deposit of gold in the region which led to the stampede of people into the region after they formalized their mining claims. This occurred when the police post which handled the claims announced the discovery.

WOMAN: Recently, a problem was discovered with a fax machine run by the Saskatoon Health Region in Saskatchewan, Canada.

The problem involves the machine not having relayed nearly 1,500 x-rays, CT, and MRI scans, ultrasounds, and other medical imaging test results to doctors. This has likely caused either delays in receiving necessary treatments or misdiagnosis, which has affected many patients.

Moreover, this problem has been found to exist for the past decade. This was revealed during a recent audit, which showed that medical diagnostic tests had been affected by the machine for at least ten years.

The machine is a part of the automated radiology information system. The audit was prompted recently when a doctor called the health region officials seeking follow-up on a patient’s report.

It was discovered that the report was sent from the system. When the staff looked into the matter, the fax machine came out to be the culprit. According to the Saskatoon Health Region spokesperson, the problem is due to a system error only rather than a staff issue.

He points out that the problem does not reflect the standard or quality in which the regional staff perform its duty.

The audit also showed that out of the 2,000,000 diagnostic tests performed, at least 1,380 had not been sent out by the machine.

The health region plans to contact each of the patients and involve their doctors to ensure that the results of the tests have been received and if there was any compromise to the patient’s care. Patients can also contact the health region via a newly set up hotline to make inquiries.

Voice7:

MAN: Sustainable development has become the number one goal in the world. Recycling should be common now, but for several reasons, it has not become so popular in Canada.

Recently, there has been a debate over implementing the polluter pays principle endorsed by United Nations members. This policy states polluters should be required to pay the costs of services if they do not recycle.

However, the response of various nationals is varied and has surfaced a substantial discussion.

Anthea Wong, sociology student of Saint Martin’s College, Edmonton, said that one of the greatest issues in the world today is the decline in the availability of resources, destruction of the environment, and depletion of energy supplies.

She added that if everyone picks up the simple habit of recycling, these environmental problems can be reduced. She advocated the importance of recycling but condemned the policy of fining those who do not recycle.

A spokesperson of Teachers Association of Ottawa, Margaret Weber, criticized the idea of imposing fines. She added that one should only be fined when one is causing inconvenience or harming others like over speeding or smoking.

Speeding on the highway is extremely dangerous and smoking causes others to suffer from secondhand smoking.

Although failing to recycle can damage the environment, it is not directly harmful to others. She concluded by questioning the fine for merely wasting resources. Lara Clark, author of Nature’s Testimony, argued that if people who don’t recycle are fined, it would be very difficult to monitor.

There are about 36,700,000 people in Canada, so there would have to be more resources allocated to catch this harmless group.

Recycling should be treated as a bonus, not as a requirement. It promotes the sustainability of our Earth, but it is unjust to fine people for refusing to save the environment.

Environmentalist Peter James Tirado said that a big reason there is global warming is that people are not recycling.

He did not completely agree with the policy of fines, but still, he emphasized that people should do something like cleaning up a park or something that will help the Earth. Also, this practice can keep the environment greener and healthier.

His counterpart, Stella Merkel, put forward her views for the motion. She said that people are often worried about financial issues, but the problem related to the environment is more serious.

Earth deserves to be given proper care and attention. The consequences of pollution are grave, and many animals are getting extinct. She said the fine is acceptable as long as it is reasonable and not taxing people’s pocket.
