WOMAN: To begin, even though wildfires have been an essential part of the ecosystem, they are becoming increasingly dangerous. MAN: I remember the one in the Yukon back in 02/2016. That was very bad. So what are these factors?

WOMAN: Well, one of the things to consider is that due to the melting of ice caps, we’re having more rain than snow in the winters. So when it comes to fire season, we have the makings of firestorms, increased temperatures, and lots of fuel.

WOMAN: It is indeed. But because we’re having longer summers, we’re also having longer winters, and this is leading to droughts in some areas of the West. Voice7:

MAN: Just recently, the federal government has imposed a controversial carbon tax that will affect the cost to consumers and businesses for even the most basic of items, from food to even medical care.

While the government states this is to help fight climate change, there seems to be more to it. As it stands, many provinces are opposing this tax. For instance, in Ontario, the provincial government is appealing against this tax in the Ontario Court of Appeals.

According to Roger Hammersmith, director of the Taxpayers Union, the group which is appealing on behalf of the Ontario government, the main argument is the overstepping of the federal government over the provinces.

He states that the government does not have the power to impose such attacks, and this has angered many of the provinces. He notes that several provincial governments are fighting this tax in the court.

Despite this, one federal government spokesperson in a recent news conference in response to these multiple suits states that the tax is a necessary and appropriate step to deal with climate change, which has been affecting the entire country.

According to the spokesperson, the tax is to curb pollution and is a charge on purchases of gasoline and other fossil fuels, and it will also be charged to those businesses which are deemed as industrial polluters. Moreover, the law only applies to those provinces that have not developed a carbon pricing scheme that meets the federal standards.

These provinces include not only Ontario, but also New Brunswick, Saskatchewan, and Manitoba. One of the biggest issues that supporters of the tax point out is that climate change is threatening the health of all Canadians and that drastic measures must be taken.

This view is supported by Hal Cuthbert, who is the president of the Public Health Council. He states that it is the federal government’s responsibility to maintain public health, and because of this, it has the right to take any action to ensure its integrity.

He points out that because climate change affects all Canadians, the carbon tax does not need to respect the purview of the provinces. This is shared by Alan Ladd, who is the associate director of Healthy Canada.

According to Ladd, because climate change affects all Canadians, it’s fair that the provinces prices rather than being left to individual provinces to decide. But even still, many groups, including those provincial governments, are contesting the tax claim that public health is already administered by the provinces.

As a result, the federal government is overstepping its reach by trampling over provincial rights.

According to Bob Ross, a member of the Canadian Government Oversight Bureau, a citizen’s rights group, the question isn’t about implied fairness by imposing an across the board tax, but rather allowing provinces the freedom to find solutions that are suitable for their own unique situations.
