However, a story from October reported the Co’s asking police to do more to stop the littering and other vandalism in the area including having signs smashed, windows broken, and fireworks set off in front of their restaurant.

According to Tommy Ko, he was overwhelmed by the support this week and in thanks put on a surprise free buffet, though customers insisted on paying anyway. Nevertheless, after fighting the harassment for some five years, the Co’s say they still are not sure they want to stay.

Voice8:

MAN: Prime Minister Trudeau recently promised new measures aimed to help Canadians buy homes during these trying times when owning a home seems to be a futile hope.

According to Trudeau, the real estate market has been afflicted by both instability and uncertainty, especially during the current economic crisis. He asserts that the current situation requires government intervention to help Canadians purchase a new home.

His plan is to pour billions of dollars into new funding, as well as to institute measures to end the practice of house flipping, the practice of buying, renovating and selling a house for profit as well as adding new regulations to police exploitative realtors.

What is compelling is that this is coming at a time when housing has become one of the top issues among Canadian voters.

The plan is committed to making home ownership available through new government funding, which includes a plan to build more homes in order to increase the housing supply, as well as taking up measures to protect the rights of buyers.

One controversial measure involves the Criminal Code. This can include prohibition on blind bidding for houses up for sale and implementing new penalties for violations. The government’s taxation powers could also be used, and the authority it has over mortgage underwriting could be used to push changes.

According to one government official speaking, on the condition of anonymity, the Criminal Code could be used to allow the government to act on violators.

The Canadian Real Estate Association, however, is highly critical of Trudeau’s plan and cites that it amounts to making any Canadian who sells their home a criminal. They argue that the current shortage in housing supply is the heart of the matter.

Moreover, they assert that Canadians have the right to choose how they wish to sell their home.

They cite that the banning of blind bidding violates this right. Trudeau took time to comment on the Conservative Party’s housing plan, which plans to build 1,000,000 new homes over three years, ease mortgage requirements, and make more federal land available for residential development.

He slammed conservative leader, Aaron O’Toole by saying that O’Toole wants to give landlords a tax break and do nothing for the renter or home buyer. He further asserts that Canadians cannot afford the Conservatives’ plan.

O’Toole countered that Trudeau should refrain from speaking, especially since he has yet to deliver after years of promises. He observed that there has been no action in the past six years to remedy the housing issue.

He claims that Trudeau has utterly failed and that Canadians deserve a solid plan rather than meaningless rhetoric and tyrannical regulation that prevents the Canadians from engaging in private enterprise and makes homes more difficult to acquire.
