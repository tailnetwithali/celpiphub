MAN: Hi. I’m Jamie Lawson. I’d like to see doctor Martin.

WOMAN: Good evening. Do you have an appointment?

MAN: Certainly. I called up in the morning to get confirmation. I know doctor Martin’s usually much occupied.

WOMAN: I’m afraid he’s not in the hospital right now. He had to rush to attend an emergency case an hour ago. A bus accident took place on the National Highway.

MAN: Oh, that’s bad. Would my appointment be rescheduled?

WOMAN: Sure. There are many people who are slated to come in even after you, and they’d be given a new timing. But I don’t know when doctor Martin would return.

MAN: What would be my appointment timings in case I prefer to leave right now? I’m still left with buying groceries and picking laundry.

WOMAN: Well, I’d have to shift all the appointments exactly by a day. So you’d have to come here tomorrow at exactly this time.

MAN: That’s a bit difficult. I already have my plans for tomorrow. My friend is leaving for England, and I’ve promised to see him off. He has his flight in the evening.

WOMAN: That’s a problem. It’d be difficult for me to readjust your timings in this case. You might have to look for another timing then as doctor Martin is occupied for the next four days.

MAN: Oh, is there nothing that can be done? I got this appointment after a lot of trouble.

WOMAN: Well, you could wait for a while. Doctor Martin may turn up anytime soon. Or if someone doesn’t show up for his or her appointment, we could accommodate you in that slot for tomorrow.

MAN: But there’s no guarantee that the doctor would be back or that someone may just skip his or her appointment for today. I guess I’d think about what to do.

WOMAN: Alright. I’d be here to assist you with whatever you decide.

Voice7:

MAN: Is having widespread advertising good for society? The question is not new. However, it has been attracting many conflicting views from people hailing from different walks of life.

Although advertising often helps in increasing the revenue generated by businesses, its effects are much more complicated than simply telling someone why they should buy particular goods and services.

Advertising is closely tied to psychology, and good marketing campaigns will engage the viewer on multiple levels. Instead of trying to make a single sale, advertising often forms a long term relationship between the consumer and the brand.

Professor Carla Gray, University of British Columbia emphasized how advertisements are doing more harm than good. She explained that people cannot just choose to ignore advertising because advertisers use many underhand methods to get their message across.

Posters have attention grabbing words or provocative pictures. Some adverts today are even being hidden in what seems like pieces of art or public information, so people do not realize they are being marketed to.

By targeting people’s unconscious thoughts, adverts are a form of brainwashing that takes away people’s freedom to make choices. Likewise, banker John Martin extended Gray’s views and added to the criticism she put forward by taking into consideration the effects of advertising on children.

He said that advertising gives the impression, especially to children, that they can and should have everything they want. This makes them too interested in material things. They are becoming more selfish and obsessed with their possessions and losing moral values and forgetting the importance of nonmaterial things like family and friends.

This harms their relationships and their personal development, which has serious effects for society on the whole. On the contrary, Lisa Parker, local cosmetic store owner, Edmonton, disapproved of both the previous views as she has tried to describe the necessity of advertising for the progress of the economy.

She said that our society is built around the idea that companies produce things that people want, and this is what makes us prosperous. She raised the concern over the loss of jobs of many, provided that consumers suddenly stopped wanting to buy so many products.

She further said that some people take materialism too far, but most people just buy what they need and then a little extra when they treat themselves. This is a much better situation than one in which people can only afford to buy things they need. That would be a step backwards.
