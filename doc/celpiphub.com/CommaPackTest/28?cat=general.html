Voice7:

WOMAN: The minimum age for obtaining a driving license should be lowered from 18 to 16, but only after a driver has received at least one hundred hours of practical training, a senior traffic officer said.

Major David Morris, the director of the Toronto Police Traffic Department, said the change would help solve the problem of untrained underage teenagers getting behind the wheel illegally.

He said to allow young people to drive at the age of 16 is a protection for them. He added that they would have the appropriate knowledge and training of how to drive safely.

He argued many teenagers drive their parents’ cars behind their backs or borrow their older siblings’ cars, making underage driving a problem. By allowing them to obtain a license after acquiring the appropriate knowledge, many lives are going to be saved.

Several driving instructors, consultants, and parents immediately opposed the idea, citing a lack of maturity among young drivers.

Peter Richardson, a general manager at the Toronto Driving Institute, said there is no evidence to suggest the average 16 year old has the maturity, judgment, or level of social responsibility necessary to interact safely on public roads.

He emphasized grave reservations with any recommendations to reduce the minimum driving age to 16 years. Simon Labette, a regional director for the Canadian Transport Research Laboratory, which is helping the Ministry of Interior establish new licensing regulations, also had reservations. He believed potential risks outweigh the benefits.

He further added that it is about their maturity, their ability to deal with underlying factors as they are still at school and learning things themselves.

Jacob Walsh, a father of a 15 year old boy, said he would not allow his son to drive at the age of 16. He noted that it was too dangerous, and his son would look at the whole driving experience as an adventure.

He further argued that one needs more maturity and experience because to drive a car is a big responsibility. Major David Morris, however, said being young did not necessarily mean being reckless and a danger on the roads.

Currently, drivers in all Toronto must be aged at least 18 to obtain a driving license, but training and testing requirements vary. Major David Morris said students should undergo twenty hours of theoretical lessons and one hundred hours of practical training before taking a driving test.

The roads and transport authority at present requires at least twenty hours of practical lessons. The Toronto Driving Institute requires seven hours of theoretical lessons and says most students need twenty to thirty hours of practical lessons before they can successfully obtain their license.
