Practice Test A - Reading Part 3: Reading for Information

Time remaining: 10 minutes

Read the following message.

A. The Saint Elias Mountains are a subgroup of the Pacific Coast Ranges, located in south-eastern Alaska in the United States, south-western Yukon and the very far north-western part of British Columbia in Canada. This mountain range was named after Mount Saint Elias which had been named in 1741 by the Danish explorer, Vitus Bering. Vitus Bering became the first official European discoverer of north-western America when he sighted the peak from his ship, the St. Peter, on July 16, 1741. The first ever ascent was made by a party led by the Duke of Abruzzi on July 31, 1897.

B. The St. Elias mountain range is an area of superlatives; it contains the largest non-polar ice-field in the world, the highest number of surging glaciers, the most seismically active mountains on earth, the largest protected area on the planet, and the highest, youngest and fastest-growing peaks in Canada. These mountains began as an area of volcanoes about 15 million years ago, when the floor of the Pacific Ocean started sinking beneath the continental margin near coastal Alaska. With this uplifting, faulting and folding of the earth’s crust, the mountains now known as the St. Elias range were born. On average, three earthquakes a day rock the earth here. In 1899, one earthquake raised the ground over 10 metres in less than two minutes. Owing to these changes, St. Elias mountain range is among the most precipitous ranges in the world.

C. The mountain range spans Wrangell-St. Elias National Park and Preserve in the United States and Kluane National Park and Reserve in Canada and includes all of Glacier Bay National Park in Alaska. In Alaska, the range includes parts of the city/borough of Yakutat and the Hoonah-Angoon and Valdez-Cordova census areas. The mountains are divided by the Duke Depression, with the shorter, more rounded Kluane Ranges to the east, and the higher Ice field Ranges to the west. Sub-ranges of Saint Elias include the Alsek Ranges, the Fairweather Range, and the Centennial Range.

D. Recent changes in climate have sped the melt of the glaciers of the St. Elias Mountains. The reduced pressure on the earth’s crust may contribute to the rapid rise of these dynamic mountains. The St. Elias Mountains rise from the shore of the north-east Pacific, blocking the wet oceanic air masses as they blow eastward. They force this air upwards, cooling it and trapping heavy precipitation, particularly winter snow. Snow accumulations of 7 metres or more is common. These enormous snow loads feed the formation of the ice-fields.

E. Not given in any of the above paragraphs.
