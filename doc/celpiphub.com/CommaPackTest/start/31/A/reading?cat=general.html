Practice Test A - Reading Part 3: Reading for Information

Time remaining: 10 minutes

Read the following message.

A. The Canadian boreal forest is a very large bio-region that extends in length from the Yukon-Alaska border right across the country to Newfoundland and Labrador. It is over 1,000 kilometres in width and separates the arctic tundra region from southern Canada. It is considered to be the largest intact forest on earth, with around three million square kilometres still undisturbed by roads, cities and industrial development. Because it is still intact it has captured the interest of both environmentalists and conservation scientists who view the untouched regions of the forest as an opportunity for large-scale conservation that would otherwise be impractical in other parts of the world.

B. The most dominant characteristic of the forest is that much of it consists of large, even-aged stands of trees. These stands contain trees of equal age and this uniformity is due to a unique cycle of natural disturbances such as forest fires, or outbreaks of pine beetle or spruce budworm that kill large tracts of forest with cyclical regularity. For instance, within the forest there are many stands of white spruce, black spruce, and balsam fir that are vulnerable to the cyclical outbreaks of the spruce budworm.

C. Ever since the melting of the great ice sheet, the boreal forest has been through many cycles of natural death. This has been the result of fire, insect outbreaks and disease. These are always followed by regeneration. Prior to European colonization of Canada and the application of modern firefighting equipment and techniques, the natural burn/regeneration cycle was less than 75 to 100 years, and it still is in many areas.

D. The forest itself is rich in water resources and contains more lakes and rivers than any comparably sized landmass on earth. It has been estimated that the region contains over 1.5 million lakes with a minimum surface area of 40,000 square metres as well as some of Canada’s largest lakes. Most large boreal lakes have cold water species of fish like trout and whitefish, while in warmer waters, species may include northern pike, walleye and smallmouth bass. There are also vast areas of wetlands. The two predominant wetland areas are the Hudson Bay Lowland and the Mackenzie River basin. Both are among the ten largest wetlands in the world and they provide wildlife habitat, maintain water flow in rivers, and store significant amounts of carbon that otherwise would be released to the atmosphere.

E. Not given in any of the above paragraphs
