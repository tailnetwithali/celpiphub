The first European drawing of the species, in the Codex Canadensis, was made by the Jesuit missionary Louis Nicolas in the early 1700s. He wrote a brief description of what he witnessed: Oumimi, or ourité, or dove. One sees such great numbers of this bird at the first passage in spring and fall that it is incredible.""

B. Passenger pigeons were easy to catch because they stayed together in large flocks and perched on the lower branches of trees. They were considered an easy source of food because they could be beaten out of their perching branches with sticks and then bagged. Unlike the domesticated carrier pigeon used for messages, these were wild birds. Even their feathers were popular for bedding; for a time in Saint-Jerome, Quebec, every dowry included a bed and pillows made of pigeon feathers. Then on September 1, 1914, Martha was found lying dead on the bottom of her cage. The passenger pigeon was now extinct. It had gone from billions of birds to zero in about a century, probably less.

The reason why the debate over keeping exotic pets has rumbled on for so long is that there is no simple answer. There are equally sound arguments on both sides of the debate, and a conclusion is hard to reach. One of the main problems in settling the debate over keeping exotic pets is the variable definition of ’exotic pets’. By the strict definition, even non-native animals that are bred locally and legally, and not taken from their natural environment, are ’exotic’. Similarly, while the term ’exotic animals’ usually sets alarm bells ringing, not all exotic animals are a threat to society. Pythons and chimpanzees, primarily, are the culprits in pet attacks, and such animals are obviously dangerous, but the extremely harmless and positively cute sugar gliders and crested geckos are not even dangerous for infants, let alone adult, sensible pet owners.

Animal rights activist, Martin Scott, said ""Most exotic pets are low-maintenance ― at least in comparison to dogs and cats. Lizards, frogs, spiders, and even snakes require basic and cheap shelters, cheap food, and minimal veterinary care. An empty aquarium with a bit of bedding and a few rocks will do just fine. Some species may need some branches and live plants. So, they are perfect for the typical urban pet owner, who can easily house these animals in an average apartment. On the flip side, though, these pets won’t be as affectionate as dogs. They will recognize you and tolerate your handling, and a few may go so far as seeking out your attention.""

In Canada, at least, the total is much less than the number of fatal attacks by dogs! That’s right; your neighbour’s Doberman Pinscher is likelier to come at you than your creepy neighbour’s boa constrictor.""

However, diseases such as hepatitis A and B, rabies, and salmonella are some commonly observable diseases that can be transmitted by exotic reptiles and mammals.""

Zoology student, Zhang Li, Edmonton University, condemned those who favoured keeping exotic animals as pets. She said that the wild populations of some exotic pets are severely affected by the pet trade. Due to this, bans have been imposed on the trade of numerous animals, but the illegal pet trade continues to fester in many wildlife-rich regions of the world. Animals such as big cats (none of which is in rude health at the moment) and many primates need all the help we can provide for their wild populations to flourish. The illegal pet trade is a big contributor to the reducing numbers of some species.
