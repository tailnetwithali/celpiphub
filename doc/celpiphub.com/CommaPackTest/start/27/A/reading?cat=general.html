Practice Test A - Reading Part 3: Reading for Information

Time remaining: 10 minutes

Read the following message.

A. The adorable animal that looks like a teddy bear, the polar bear is the only bear considered to be a marine mammal because it depends upon the marine environment for survival. Polar bears spend most of their lives on sea ice, which they use as a platform to hunt their favourite food: ringed seals. A keen sense of smell is key to the polar bear’s success as a hunter, and these bears can detect a seal’s breathing hole in the ice from up to a kilometre away. Additionally, polar bears are opportunistic as well as predatory: they will consume dead fish and carcasses of stranded whales and eat garbage near human settlements. They have no natural predators and knows no fear of humans, making it an extremely dangerous animal.

B. Its long body, neck and skull distinguish it from other types of bears. They have a water-repellent coat that insulates them from the cold air and water. In fact, the fur keeps them so warm that adult males can quickly overheat when they run. In addition to 2 layers of fur, a thick layer of fat helps keep the bears warm. A polar bear’s body fat can measure up to 11.4 cm (4.49 in). Polar bear paws are ideal for getting around in the Arctic. The male, which is much larger than the female, weighs 410 to 720 kg (900 to 1,600 pounds). It grows to about 1.6 metres (5.3 feet) tall at the shoulder and 2.2–2.5 metres in length. The broad feet have hairy soles to protect and insulate as well as to facilitate movement across ice, as does the uneven skin on the soles of the feet, which helps to prevent slipping.

C. In Canada, polar bears can be found from James Bay to northern Ellesmere Island, and from Labrador to the Alaskan border. Churchill, Manitoba, on the western coast of Hudson Bay, is one of the three largest polar bear maternity denning areas in the world. Canada is one of five ’’polar bear nations,’’ along with the United States (Alaska), Russia, Denmark (Greenland) and Norway. Residents of Churchill, Canada share their streets with the largest land carnivore in the world. Owing to this a lot of stories float around in the town, like opening the drapes to a noise and seeing a bear looking back at you through the window. Such is everyday life, particularly during October and November, in this small town.

D. Apart from these real life encounters, there are many other stories that have been passed down from generation to generation. They view the Polar Bear as a mystic creature that is very spiritual in nature. It is one of the animals they show the most admiration for among their culture. Some of these stories talk about the Polar Bears covering their noses with their paws while hunting. There are also many stories and legends that involve human souls coming to visit their loved ones in form of Polar Bears. As a result, you will see that many cultures have quite a bit of respect for these grand creatures. A terrific family film called the Golden Compass, is based on this theme. It is a screenplay that has been based on a best-selling Novel by the same name.

E. Not given in any of the above paragraphs.
