Hi Ron, I was thrilled to know that you have joined a new company recently. I understand that starting a new job can be stressful. You’re in an unfamiliar environment and finding your place in it will be the difference between excelling at your job or crashing and burning. So I would like to give you some advice that can ease the transition and help you adjust in your new workplace. You will be taking in an enormous amount of new information during your first few weeks on the job, from understanding the work culture to learning the ropes of your job. You will not retain all of this information in the beginning, and that’s normal. In most jobs, it takes anywhere from three to six months to feel like you know what you’re doing, and in some especially complicated jobs, even longer. That feeling nearly always goes away, but it takes time. Meanwhile keep talking to your colleagues and reach out to them to in case of any confusion. This will help them accept you as a team member more easily and quickly. I wish you luck, I hope you do great and have an amazing time in the new environment, among new colleagues and superiors.

Last summer, I decided to spend my vacations at my ancestral village in Uttar Pradesh, India. I had never been there before and wanted to experience the lifestyle I had studied about in school. I can’t paint a rosy picture of village life, because I witnessed much scope for development there. But I do have some pleasant memories. The mornings there were beautiful and calming unlike the city where there is never a moment of silence. During a morning stroll, I even saw peacocks strutting in the fields. The people there were quite vocal in voicing their opinions. One day I went to have some tea in a rundown stall; I could overhear disgruntled men discussing the contemporary issues of the society quite loudly. It seemed as if they would get into a physical fight. But after a few minutes they were all laughing and having tea together. The tea stall owner told me that this was nothing new and he had never seen the people letting their grudges or disagreements affect their relationships. What I did not like there was the power-cuts in the evenings. But that also turned into a pleasant experience. People would come out of their houses to enjoy the cool breeze and catch up with their friends. This was quite different from in cities where people don’t even know who lives next door.
